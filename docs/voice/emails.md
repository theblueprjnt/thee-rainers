# Emails

How I write emails. Two registers: broadcasts (one to many) and one-to-one replies.

## Broadcasts (lead nurture, customer updates)

The same voice as the website, slightly warmer. The reader gave me their email, so I owe them more than a stranger gets.

### Shape

- Subject line, 4 to 8 words. No emoji, no clickbait. The subject is a promise. The email keeps it.
- Open with a single sentence that earns the read. Either a diagnosis the reader recognises or a stake in the ground.
- Body is short paragraphs. One idea per paragraph, two to four lines max.
- One ask per email. One link, sometimes two. Never three.
- Sign off plain. My name on its own line.

### Do

- Start the email mid-thought, as if the reader and I have been talking. "The reason most stances break under pressure is..."
- Use the same locked names as the website. Workshop, Blueprint, Private Architecture, Greatness Community, Foot Space Work.
- Keep the link copy direct: "Reserve the workshop" not "Click here to learn more."
- Make the value of the email standalone. If they never click the link, the email still gave them something.

### Do not

- Do not write a long introduction. Land the first sentence and go.
- Do not write a P.S. with a different ask. It dilutes the one ask.
- Do not use marketing words: "exclusive," "limited time," "don't miss out."
- Do not start with "Hey [name], hope you're doing well." Strangers and friends both find that hollow.

### Example shape

**Subject:** What gets fixed first

**Body (three short paragraphs):**

Most fighters try to fix speed and end up fighting their own stance.

The Workshop on June 27 covers what to fix and in what order. Camera on, ninety minutes, you leave with the four bases drilled in your own body.

Reserve the seat → [link]

Rainers

## One-to-one replies (DM-style)

Warm. Brief. Organise the conversation and close the loop. This is where I sound the most like myself.

### Do

- Greet by name if I know it.
- Match their energy. If they wrote two lines, write two lines. If they wrote a paragraph, write a paragraph.
- Light emoji is fine in one-to-one. A single 🥊 or 🙏 at the end is on-brand. Never strings of emoji.
- Use "just" and "actually" — those words break the machine rhythm. Keep them.
- End with a next step. "Reply with your record and what is breaking down." or "Watch the loom, then tell me where you got stuck."

### Do not

- Do not paste website-style declarative blocks into a reply. It reads cold.
- Do not pitch in the first reply unless they asked. Ask the diagnostic question first.
