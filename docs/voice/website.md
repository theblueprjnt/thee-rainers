# Website Copy

How I write for theerainers.com. Landing pages, product pages, sales pages, anything publicly readable on the site.

## The job

The site converts traffic into a lead or a customer. Every block of copy on it serves one of those two outcomes. Free value (teaching, content, examples) lives on socials, not the site. The site diagnoses, frames, and invites. It does not teach the curriculum before the sale.

## Register

Owner-grade. Calm. Slightly colder than how I talk in a DM, because the reader is meeting me for the first time. Authority without warmth on the first scroll. Warmth earned by the third.

## Rules of construction

- Short, declarative, period-ended sentences. Most under twelve words.
- Lead the paragraph with the cleanest, strongest sentence. Earn attention before asking for anything.
- Physical language. "Your stance breaks." Not "your foundation is suboptimal."
- Diagnose the trap better than the reader can name it. If the reader thinks *yes that is me*, the rest of the page does its job.
- Faith woven, never preached. 1 Corinthians 9:26 is the spine. Quote scripture as a coda after the final CTA, never next to a price.
- Dead simple English. A real share of the tribe reads through a translation app. Clever phrasing dies in translation. Concrete physical words survive.

## Headlines

- Two words to seven, max. "Engineered for the ring." "Live with me. Every week."
- Period at the end. Never an exclamation mark.
- Never a rhetorical question as a section header.
- The hero headline is the one promise the page makes. Everything below it earns the promise.

## Subheads

- One sentence that adds a specific fact the headline could not carry. Format, length, frequency, scope.
- Stays at the same physical altitude as the headline. No abstraction allowed under a concrete headline.

## Body

- Two to four sentences per block. Past that, split into a new block.
- One block, one idea. Do not stack.
- Names of things repeat verbatim every time. Workshop, Blueprint, Private Architecture, Greatness Community, Foot Space Work. Never paraphrase a system name to "sound different."
- No "not X, it is Y." That construction reads as defensive. Replace with the positive statement of what it *is*.

## CTAs

- Verb plus thing plus arrow. "Reserve Your Seat · $197 →" not "Find Out More."
- Price visible on every paid CTA. Hiding the price wastes the click.
- Free CTAs say "Free" in the label. The colour token tells half the story; the word closes it.
- Middle dot ` · ` separator. Never a hyphen or em dash.

## Do

- Lead with the constraint, not the product. "Most fighters trying to move faster are fighting their own stance." Then offer the product as the fix.
- Name the face. "Camera on." "Live with me." "I correct your movement." Singular *I* belongs to the brand voice when the page is about the work I do.
- Prefer concrete numbers to vague intensifiers. "90 minutes." Not "an in-depth session."
- Close with scripture as a coda, in a dark section, after the final CTA.

## Do not

- Do not start a section with a rhetorical question.
- Do not state numeric buyer counts ("114 fighters bought this"). Small numbers anti-sell, big numbers feel inflated. Use proof density instead: "Fighters and coaches across the world train this system."
- Do not say "masterclass," "calibration," or any "real X" construction.
- Do not stack three CTAs at equal visual weight on a thank-you page. Pick the one that matches what they just did.
- Do not put a fifth optional offer next to a paid moment. Calm secondary, never competing primary.

## Example bank

**Hero promise (Workshop):** "Camera on. Real-time correction on your defensive structure, movement patterns, and mechanical foundation. You leave with a framework you run again and again."

**Headline shape:** "Engineered for the ring." "Live with me. Every week." "Wrong corner."

**Subhead shape:** "The foundation most gyms skip. Step by step."

**Quiet next-step (post-purchase):** "Know which lever is holding your system back. The 7-lever audit takes 3 minutes."

**Scripture coda:** "I do not run aimlessly; I do not box as one beating the air." — 1 Corinthians 9:26
