# Communication Principles

The principles under every surface. If a sentence does not pass these, rewrite it before publishing.

## One register, five flavours

The same person writes the website, the email, the caption, the DM, the voice note. Different distances. Same voice.

## The seven principles

### 1. Diagnose before you offer

Name the trap before you name the product. The reader has to think *yes that is me* before they trust the fix. "Most fighters trying to move faster are fighting their own stance." Then offer the work.

### 2. Concrete physical language

Movement words. "Stance breaks." "Weight drops." "Chain disconnects." Avoid abstract management words. "Optimise," "leverage," "calibrate" all fail this test.

### 3. Short, declarative, period-ended

Most sentences under twelve words. Period at the end. The rhythm builds momentum and survives translation.

### 4. Faith woven, never preached

1 Corinthians 9:26 is the spine. Scripture is a coda after the work, never an opener and never a substitute for the work. Most copy never quotes scripture. The work itself is the sermon.

### 5. Dead simple English

A real share of the tribe reads through a translation app. Clever phrasing dies in translation. Concrete words and short sentences survive. When in doubt, say it the simplest way.

### 6. Earn before you ask

The first paragraph diagnoses or shows. The CTA comes after the reader knows I see the problem they have. Never lead with the ask.

### 7. Substance under every claim

If I write "this works," there is a specific reason it works directly under it. The brand is technical. Claims pay rent in specifics.

## Banned permanently

- Em dashes —
- En dashes –
- Exclamation marks !
- Rhetorical questions as section headers
- "Not X, it is Y" standalone constructions
- "Masterclass," "calibration," "real X"
- Numeric buyer counts ("114 fighters bought this")
- Hype, hyperbole, urgency theatre

## Locked names (always exactly)

Workshop. Blueprint. Private Architecture. Greatness Community. Foot Space Work.

Never paraphrase a system name to "sound different." Consistency is what builds the brand mark in the reader's head.

## Mine, keep them

- "just"
- "actually"
- "honestly"

These small human words break the machine rhythm. They are the difference between me writing and a tool writing. Keep them where they earn their seat.

## How to check a draft

Read aloud. If I run out of breath, split the sentence. If a sentence sounds like a brochure, rewrite it as something I would say. If a paragraph could be by any coach, make it specifically mine — diagnostic, physical, direct.
